**Word count:** About 394 words of story (transcribed to SCR/story.txt), plus about 110 in the afterword.

**Form:**
- Rhyming anapestic couplets modeled on "'Twas the Night Before Christmas".
- It opens with "'Twas the night before Christmas, and all through the land…" and ends with "Merry Christmas to all, and to all a good night!"

**Reading level (inferred):**
- Read-aloud for ages 3–8. Independent reading around grades 2–3.
- Some vocabulary is archaic: saith, nigh, foretold, deceived.
- A live-site review mentions a 4-year-old.

**Setting:**
- Ancient America in a Mesoamerican style: step pyramids like El Castillo, thatched huts, jungle, waterfalls.
- The source is **3 Nephi 1:4–22**, and the website says "inspired by 3 Nephi 1":
  - unbelievers set a day to kill the believers (v.9)
  - Nephi bows down and cries to the Lord (v.11–12)
  - "Lift up your head and be of good cheer… on the morrow come I into the world" (v.13)
  - there is no darkness that night (v.15)
  - the unbelievers fall to the earth (v.16–17)
  - a new star appears (v.21)
- It also draws on Samuel the Lamanite's prophecy in Helaman 14:2–5, a day and a night as one day.

**Story, page by page:**
- p3–p9: On the night before Christmas the wicked grow threatening. Samuel the prophet said the sign would soon come, but the wait feels too long for some. The believers pray while the unbelievers mock them ("The Savior's not coming; your faith is in vain…").
- p10–p11: The believers keep watching in faith for "a night without darkness, with no setting sun."
- p12–p13: A proclamation is nailed to a tree: those who believe "shall be put to death if the sign… does not come TONIGHT!"
- p14–p17: Nephi the prophet prays in a grove outside town, and the Lord comforts him: "Son, lift up your head, and be of good cheer!"
- p18–p19: Christ speaks: "For of Mary tomorrow I shall be born, in Bethlehem of Judea…"
- p20–p23: That night no darkness comes. The faithful are saved and the unbelievers fall to the earth, ashamed.
- p24–p26: Jacob gives his Nativity drawing to the bald unbeliever and they hug. The closing message is "remember Christ came to save all of us, too! So never lose faith! Keep Him always in sight!"

**Afterword (p28):**
- It explains that prophets foretold Jesus' birth, and says "We are all children of God!"
- It invites the child to find the Christ symbols: the DOVE (peace) and the STAR (light, guidance), with "How many stars can you find?"

**Characters:**
- **Jacob:** the boy in the red headband and teal tunic, the POV child who is always drawing. He is not named in the story text. The name comes from the coloring book ("Help Jacob Find Nephi" maze, and a waving Jacob on the cover).
- **Nephi the prophet:** white-haired and bearded, light robes, holds gold plates.
- **Samuel the prophet:** referenced in the text but not clearly depicted.
- **Jacob's family (inferred):** a mother in a red dress, a little sister in pink with pigtails, a young man in a headband (older brother or father), and an elderly woman. A pregnant woman appears on p3 and p24, perhaps as a parallel to Mary.
- **Antagonist:** a bald, black-bearded, armored unbeliever with a sword. He posts the proclamation, mocks the believers, falls ashamed, and is embraced by Jacob. Other unbelievers include a woman in a feathered headdress.

**Themes:**
- Faith while waiting, and prayer that is answered.
- Prophecy fulfilled.
- Christ's birth witnessed in the Americas on the same night as Bethlehem.
- Forgiveness and inclusion: the unbeliever is embraced.
- Christ came for everyone.

**Illustration style:**
- Digital storybook cartoon: clean black line art, soft painterly and textured color, big-eyed "chibi" children.
- Warm earthy palette, with dramatic light in the night and grove scenes.
- A hidden dove on nearly every page, which a review confirms ("the kids love finding the dove").

**Commands for spreads.** The page is 1919.84 pt wide, so `-r 150` renders at the native 4000×1630:
```
SRC="/Users/ryanmata/Desktop/Arrowleaf Marketing/tiffany-wheeler/assets/media-library/Spreads-Nephite-Christmas.pdf"
pdftoppm -r 150 -f $n -l $n -singlefile -png "$SRC" tmp/s$n
magick tmp/s$n.png -resize 2000x -strip -quality 82 -sampling-factor 4:2:0 -interlace JPEG peek/nc-peek-XX.jpg
cwebp -quiet -q 80 -resize 2000 0 tmp/s$n.png -o peek/nc-peek-XX.webp
```
Run this for n in 4, 7, 10, 11, 12, 15.

**Single pages** are only 411 pt wide, so they must be rendered with `-r 350` to get the native 2000×1610. At `-r 150` they come out only 858 px wide.

**If the pages PDF turns out to be the final text,** build each spread from its two pages:
```
pdftoppm -r 350 -f L -l L …pages… a
pdftoppm -r 350 -f R -l R …pages… b
magick a.png b.png +append -resize 2000x out.jpg
```
