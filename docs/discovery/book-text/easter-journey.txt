Shalom!
Welcome to Easter Journey!
We invite you to step back in time, through the gates of
Jerusalem, and meet some of the people who knew and
loved Jesus. You’ll hear from those who walked with
Him and witnessed the events of the Easter story as
they unfolded.
As you listen to their stories, think about how you
would have felt if you were there. Would you have
believed His miracles? Would you have followed Him?
Would you have been willing to testify that Jesus is the
Promised Messiah, even at the risk of death?
This story is interactive, so if someone asks who you are
looking for, you can say, “We seek Jesus of Nazareth.”
And if they ask if you are His friends, say, “Yes.” At
the end of each part, they will say, “Shalom.” You are
welcome to say, “Shalom,” back.
Now let’s enter Jerusalem and see if we can find Jesus
of Nazareth.

Streets of Jerusalem
Merchant: Ahh, welcome! Welcome to our humble city.
What brings you to Jerusalem, weary traveler? Have you
come for the Passover Feast? Or maybe you have come
seeking Jesus of Nazareth?
Many people have come from the surrounding hills to hear
His preaching. Some just want to see His mighty miracles.
Have you heard of the miracles of Jesus? He walks the roads of
Jerusalem, healing the sick, causing the blind to see, and even
raising the dead. And yet there are many who still say Jesus is
just a man. Others say He is a prophet. Some, like myself, believe
He is the true Messiah.
I, like all Jews, have watched and waited for the day when the
Messiah would come to deliver His people. I am not sure what I
expected—maybe a powerful king like David or Solomon, not a
simple carpenter from Nazareth. Yet I have watched Jesus’ miracles
in the streets of Jerusalem. I have heard Him teach near the temple.
His teachings bring a new peace to my soul.

Outside the Doors of the Temple
Blind Man: Good Passover, my friends. It is late, and you look lost.
Do you know whom you seek?
(We seek Jesus of Nazareth.)
Jesus of Nazareth, you say? There are many that are not happy
with Jesus today. His teachings and miracles threaten them. Today,
Jesus entered the temple and saw people buying and selling things.
He was upset they had turned the temple into a “den of thieves.”
He turned over their tables and let their animals go, as a reminder
that the temple is a house of prayer, not a place for business. If
they didn’t feel threatened before, they do now and many want to
silence Him!
But not me. Jesus healed me.
You see, I was born blind. I lived in darkness and spent my days
begging on the steps of the temple. Most people walked by and
didn’t notice me. But one day, Jesus came. I sensed something was
different about this man. I felt Him lean down next to me, and His
fingers touched my face as He gently rubbed something over my
eyes. It didn’t hurt. It felt cool and soft, like mud. Then He told me
to “Go and wash in the pool of Siloam.”
Now, the pool of Siloam was on the other side of the city from
where I sat. Practically impossible for a blind man to get there. But
I believed His words and I wanted to be healed! I made my way
there, and as I entered the pool, I slowly started to wash the mud
from my eyes. For a moment, I didn’t dare open them. What if I
had hoped for too much? But as the mud fell from my eyes, tears
started to run down my cheeks. Light and color overtook me, and
the darkness disappeared. I could see!

Mary: Oh, friends. I hear you are friends of Jesus.

have just returned from the hill Golgotha, where
they have crucified my Son.

As we reached Golgotha, I felt as if a sword had

A mother should never be asked to watch her son

Can You Find These Symbols of
Christ Throughout this Book?
Cross – The cross reminds us that Jesus gave His life for
everyone. Because He loves us so much, He died to take
away our sins and then rose again, so we can have new
life with Him.
Easter Lily – The white petals remind us that Jesus is
pure and holy, and the flower blooms around Easter
to celebrate that He rose from the dead, giving us new
life and hope. They represent His service, sacrifice, and
invitation to “come unto Him.”
Oil Lamp – Light represents truth and guidance. Christ
is the “light of the world” (John 8:12) and gives spiritual
oil that keeps our lamps burning.
Dove – Represents the peace that Christ brings us, for
He is the Prince of Peace!
Fig – Fig trees often symbolize spiritual fruitfulness.
Christ cursed the barren fig tree, showing that He seeks
genuine righteousness, not just the appearance of it.
Pelican – In medieval Christian tradition, the pelican
was believed to pierce its own breast to feed its young
with its blood. This symbolizes Christ’s self-sacrifice,
offering His own life and blood for humanity.
Palm Leaves – Waved at Christ’s triumphal entry into
Jerusalem (John 12:13), symbolizing victory, kingship,
and eternal life.
Door Knocker – Christ stands at the door and knocks
(Revelation 3:20). It represents His invitation to enter
our lives, but we must choose to open.

Easter Journey Advent
Use this as a daily reflection at the end of each story segment. Look for the fig
symbol behind the page number to know when a section has ended.
Sunday - Day One: Intro & Merchant (Pages 1-5)
• How was Jesus a king?
• What does it mean that He is the Promised Messiah?
• How would you have felt if the Messiah finally arrived after waiting so many
years?
Monday - Day Two: Blind Man (Pages 6-9)
• What does it mean to be spiritually blind?
• Do miracles still happen today?
• Have you ever been healed —physically, emotionally, or spiritually?
Tuesday - Day Three: Last Supper (Pages 10-15)
• Jesus washed His disciples’ feet as an act of love and service.
• How can we love and serve others as He has commanded?
Wednesday - Day Four: Gethsemane (Pages 16-19)
• Can you imagine Jesus praying for you?
• What are some ways you have felt Jesus’ love in your life?
Thursday - Day Five: Caiaphas’ Home (Pages 20-25)
• How would you have felt that night in Caiaphas’ house?
• Would you have stood up to defend Jesus, even if it meant danger or death:
• What are ways we are asked to “stand up for Jesus” in our lives today?
Friday - Day Six: Mary, His Mother (Pages 26-31)
• How do you think those who knew Jesus felt when they learned He was
crucified?
• How can you trust God when you don’t understand why difficult things
happen?
Saturday - Day Seven: Christ in the Tomb (No Reading)
• Have you ever felt hopeless or extremely sad, unsure how things would get
better?
• How does Christ give us hope, even in difficult or sad times?
Sunday - Day Eight: Angels & Mary in the Garden (Pages 32-41)
• Share your testimony of the Savior.
• How can you share the joy of Easter with others in your family or community?
